Charles, Pia, and Yichen are in London for NIME2026 presenting and performing works from the ANU Sound Music and Creative Computing Lab ([SMCClab](https://smcclab.au/))!

![回溯: Co-constructing a Dual Feedback Apparatus by Yichen Wang and Charles Martin]({% link assets/2026-nime-coconstructing.jpg %})

ANU and the SMCClab is well represented at NIME this year! We started by presenting the [Future NIME Leaders Workshop](https://nime2026.org/proceedings/658.html) yesterday, a new initiative to surface and empower NIMErs who want to contribute to the community beyond submitting and reviewing.

Over the rest of the conference we have 

- [Histories of the Schmitt Trigger in Handmade Electronic Instruments for Making Sound in the Arts: A Literature Review](https://nime2026.org/proceedings/485.html) (Pia van Gelder) - Poster Session 1.
- [Opening the Design Space: Two Years of Performance with Intelligent Musical Instruments](https://nime2026.org/proceedings/86.html) (Charles Martin) - Papers 9B
- [A Web Interface for Real-Time Interaction with Machine Learning in Musical Performance](https://nime2026.org/proceedings/196.html) (Hongzhe Zhu; Charles Martin) - Poster Session 3 (remote)
- [回溯：Co-constructing a Dual Feedback Apparatus](https://nime2026.org/proceedings/199.html) (Yichen Wang; Charles Martin) - Concert 8

In Charles' paper he will talk about learning how to create intelligent musical instruments with [IMPSYpi platform](https://charlesmartin.au/impsy/) and Charles will play an IMPSY instrument in the Friday evening concert.

The new news is that IMPSY is now available as an [AUv3 MIDI effect](https://charlesmartin.au/impsy-auv3/) and a [web application](https://charlesmartin.au/impsy-web/), so it’s easier than ever to engage with real-time, small-data, AI in musical interaction. Give it a try!
